The Problem I'm Working On

Erdös Problem 106

Draw \( n \) squares inside the unit square with no common interior point. Let \( f(n) \) be the maximum possible sum of the side-lengths of the squares. Is \( f(k^2 + 1) = k \)?

Current thoughts / Sketch:

It's easy to show \(f(k^2 + 1) \geq k\) and it's conjectured that \(f(k^2 + 1) = k\) holds. Baek et al. showed that if we further assume the sides of all squares are parallel to the sides of the unit square, then the maximum total side length of \(k^2 + 1\) squares is \(k\). See arXiv:2411.07274.
